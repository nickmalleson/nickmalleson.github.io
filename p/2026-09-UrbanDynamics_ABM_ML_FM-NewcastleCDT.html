ABSTRACT: Modelling Urban Dynamics with Agents, Machine Learning, and Foundation Models Cities are complex systems whose behaviour is driven by a multitude of diverse, interacting entities that operate across multiple spatial and temporal scales and for whom high-quality data are rarely available at the level required. This presents enormous challenges for modelling. This talk will present a range of contemporary approaches that can be used to better understand and, in some cases, predict outcomes in cities. It begins by illustrating how machine learning techniques, coupled with high-resolution pedestrian count data, can offer insight into urban dynamics: where people are, where they are likely to be, and how what these changing patterns can tell us about wider urban evolution. It will then discuss how innovations in foundation modelling and the transformer architecture can be used to draw insight from ‘big’ data sources such as images of places and user-generated text describing neighbourhoods. Finally it turns to agent-based modelling, using examples from research on crime to show how individual behaviour can be simulated directly, and considers what these three approaches might offer one another.
